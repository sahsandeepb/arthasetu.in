How Section 80D Deductions Work in India

Under Section 80D of the Income Tax Act, individual taxpayers and HUFs can claim income deductions for health insurance premiums paid via non-cash modes (net banking, debit/credit cards, UPI, cheques). Cash payments are only permitted for preventive health check-ups up to ₹5,000.

Statutory Limits Matrix:

  • Self, Spouse & Dependent Children (Below 60): Maximum deduction of ₹25,000.
  • Self or Spouse (Senior Citizen 60+): Enhanced limit of ₹50,000.
  • Parents (Below 60): Additional deduction up to ₹25,000.
  • Parents (Senior Citizens 60+): Enhanced additional deduction up to ₹50,000.
  • Maximum Combined Potential: Up to ₹1,00,000 in a single financial year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can medical expenses of senior citizen parents be claimed without insurance?

Yes. If your senior citizen parents (age 60 or above) do not have active health insurance coverage, you can claim medical treatment expenses (consultation, hospitalisation, medicines) up to ₹50,000 under Section 80D.

Is GST on health insurance eligible for deduction?

Yes. The total premium amount paid, inclusive of GST (currently 18%), is eligible for deduction under Section 80D up to the statutory threshold.

Can in-laws' health insurance premiums be claimed under Section 80D?

No. Premiums paid for mother-in-law or father-in-law cannot be claimed by the son-in-law or daughter-in-law. Only biological or adoptive parents qualify.
