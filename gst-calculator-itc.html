The Mathematics of GST: Exclusive vs Inclusive

Understanding the exact arithmetic difference between exclusive and inclusive GST is crucial to avoid under-billing or tax penalties:

  • GST Exclusive (Forward Charge): Applied when quoting base rates without taxes. Formula: GST = (Base Price × Rate) / 100; Final Invoice Total = Base Price + GST.
  • GST Inclusive (Backward Calculation): Applied when prices are inclusive of all taxes (e.g. MRP). Formula: Base Price = Gross Amount / (1 + (Rate / 100)); Tax Component = Gross Amount - Base Price.

How Input Tax Credit (ITC) Offsets GSTR-3B Cash Liability

Input Tax Credit allows a registered business to avoid the cascading effect of "tax on tax". If you generate sales with ₹18,000 output GST, but paid ₹8,000 GST on raw materials and server bills during the same month, your net payable liability in Form GSTR-3B is reduced to just ₹10,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between CGST, SGST, and IGST?

When goods or services are supplied within the same state (Intra-State), the tax is split equally between the Central Government (CGST) and State Government (SGST). For supply across state borders or exports (Inter-State), 100% of the tax is collected as Integrated GST (IGST).

Can ITC be claimed if the supplier does not file GSTR-1?

No. Under Section 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act, you can only claim Input Tax Credit if the invoice appears in your auto-generated Form GSTR-2B, confirming that your supplier has filed their returns.

What happens if my available ITC exceeds output GST?

If your available ITC is greater than your output GST liability, your net cash tax payable for that month is ₹0. The excess credit carries forward indefinitely in your Electronic Credit Ledger to offset future tax liabilities.
