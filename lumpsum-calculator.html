The Mathematics of Lump Sum Compounding

A lump sum investment benefits from 100% uninterrupted compounding right from Day 1. Every year, returns are generated not just on the original principal, but on all previous accumulated gains. The standard compounding formula used across mutual funds and asset managers is: A = P × (1 + r)^t, where P represents principal, r is the annual rate of return, and t is the duration in years.

Taxation on One-Time Equity Investments (Section 112A)

When you redeem an equity mutual fund held for more than 12 months, the profit is classified as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG):

  • Statutory Exemption: The first ₹1,25,000 of profit redeemed in a financial year is completely tax-free.
  • Flat 12.5% Tax Rate: Gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh are taxed at 12.5% (plus 4% health & education cess = 13.0% effective tax).
  • Indexation Benefit: Equity funds do not get cost indexation benefits under current tax laws.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is lump sum better than SIP during a market correction?

Yes. If the market undergoes a significant correction (10% to 15% dip from recent highs), deploying surplus cash as a lump sum gives you lower average acquisition cost and higher long-term compounding upside compared to spreading it out.

What is real inflation-adjusted purchasing power?

If your ₹5 Lakh investment grows to ₹19.7 Lakhs in 10 years, inflation (e.g. 6% p.a.) simultaneously increases the price of goods. Inflation-adjusted value tells you what that future ₹19.7 Lakhs will actually be able to buy in today's currency terms.

Can I withdraw my lump sum investment in parts?

Yes. You can withdraw your money through a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) or partial redemptions. Planning partial annual withdrawals under the ₹1.25 Lakh LTCG limit allows you to enjoy tax-free gains over multiple financial years.
