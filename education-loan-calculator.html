Section 80E: 100% of education loan interest is tax-deductible for 8 consecutive years with NO upper ceiling.

The Unique Power of Section 80E Tax Deductions

Unlike Section 80C (capped at ₹1.5 Lakh) or Section 24(b) (home loan interest capped at ₹2 Lakh), Section 80E provides 100% unlimited tax deduction on the interest component paid on higher education loans. Whether your annual interest is ₹80,000 or ₹4,00,000, the entire amount is deducted from gross taxable income.

Moratorium Period & Simple Interest

Most Indian public and private sector banks offer a repayment moratorium encompassing the course duration plus 6 to 12 months. If you service simple interest during the study period, you prevent interest capitalization, significantly reducing the post-graduation EMI and total loan burden.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum period for claiming Section 80E?

You can claim deductions under Section 80E for a maximum of 8 continuous assessment years, starting from the year repayment begins, or until the entire interest is paid off—whichever is earlier.

Are education loans from private lenders or NBFCs eligible?

The loan must be sanctioned by a banking company, approved financial institution, or recognized charitable organization. Loans borrowed from relatives, friends, or unapproved private money lenders do NOT qualify.

Can Section 80E be claimed under the New Tax Regime?

No. Deductions under Section 80E are available exclusively under the Old Tax Regime. The New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) disallows Section 80E claims in exchange for lower base slab tax rates.
