Rules for Deducting TDS on Property under Section 194-IA

According to Section 194-IA of the Income Tax Act, any buyer purchasing an immovable property (residential house, flat, commercial shop, or non-agricultural land) worth ₹50,00,000 or more must deduct 1% TDS before releasing payment to the resident seller.

Higher of Sale Price vs Circle Rate Rule

Under statutory amendments, the 1% TDS is computed on the higher of the actual sale consideration or the stamp duty value (circle rate). If a property is purchased for ₹55 Lakhs but the circle rate is ₹60 Lakhs, TDS must be deducted on ₹60 Lakhs (i.e. ₹60,000).

Important Steps for Property Buyers:

  • TAN is Not Required: Individual buyers do not need a TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number). Form 26QB can be filed using standard PAN or Aadhaar.
  • Form 16B Certificate: After depositing the TDS amount with the government via TIN-NSDL / Protean portal, the buyer must download Form 16B from TRACES and provide it to the seller as proof.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does 1% TDS apply if 2 buyers buy a property for ₹60 Lakhs (₹30L each)?

Yes. As per judicial precedents and CBDT clarifications, the ₹50 Lakh threshold applies to the entire property transaction value, not individual shares. Since the property value is ₹60 Lakhs, TDS applies.

Is agricultural land subject to Section 194-IA TDS?

No. Agricultural land situated in rural India is completely exempt from TDS under Section 194-IA. However, urban non-agricultural land is fully subject to 1% TDS.

What happens if the buyer fails to deduct or deposit TDS?

Failure to deduct TDS attracts interest at 1% per month under Section 201(1A), and failure to deposit deducted TDS attracts 1.5% interest per month. Late filing of Form 26QB attracts a non-negotiable penalty of ₹200 per day under Section 234E.
