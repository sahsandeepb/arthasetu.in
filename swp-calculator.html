How a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) Works in India

An SWP allows an investor to redeem a specific rupee amount from a mutual fund scheme on a fixed day every month. Unlike selling the entire corpus, only the units required to generate your monthly withdrawal are redeemed. The remaining units stay invested in the market, continuing to generate compound interest and dividend growth.

Tax Efficiency: SWP vs Bank FD Interest

In a bank Fixed Deposit, the entire annual interest payout is fully taxable at your income tax slab rate under "Income from Other Sources". In contrast, with an SWP, each withdrawal consists of two parts: Principal Return (completely non-taxable) and Capital Gains. Only the capital gains portion is subject to tax (equity LTCG is tax-exempt up to ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year, and taxed at 12.5% beyond that), resulting in significantly lower tax outgo for retirees.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my SWP balance become zero?

Yes. If your monthly withdrawal amount is significantly higher than the fund's annual rate of return, your capital will gradually decrease each month until the corpus is fully exhausted. Keeping your annual withdrawal rate below or equal to your expected return preserves the principal indefinitely.

Can I increase or pause my SWP amount midway?

Yes. Systematic Withdrawal Plans are fully flexible. You can modify your withdrawal amount, pause the payouts, or withdraw a lumpsum amount whenever needed without penalty.

Which mutual fund categories are best suited for an SWP?

Conservative Hybrid Funds, Balanced Advantage / Dynamic Asset Allocation Funds, and Equity Savings Funds are widely considered optimal for SWPs as they balance periodic market volatility with long-term capital preservation.
