How Section 44ADA Works for Freelancers and Consultants

Section 44ADA is a presumptive taxation scheme under the Income Tax Act designed specifically for specified professionals: software developers, IT consultants, designers, doctors, chartered accountants, lawyers, architects, and technical consultants. Instead of maintaining every expense receipt, the law presumes 50% of your gross billing as deductible business expenses, taxing only the remaining 50% profit.

Turnover Threshold & Digital Receipt Rule

The standard ceiling for Section 44ADA is ₹50,00,000. However, the limit is enhanced to ₹75,00,000 if your aggregate cash receipts do not exceed 5% of total gross receipts during the financial year. Payments received via bank transfer, NEFT/RTGS, UPI, or credit cards qualify as digital receipts.

Advance Tax Relaxation for Section 44ADA

While regular companies and sole proprietors must pay advance tax in 4 installments throughout the year (June, September, December, March), professionals filing under Section 44ADA are required to pay 100% of their advance tax in a single payment on or before 15th March.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim actual expenses if they exceed 50% of turnover?

Yes. If your actual business expenses exceed 50% of your gross receipts and your profit is lower, you can declare lower profit. However, doing so makes it mandatory to maintain formal books of accounts under Section 44AA and undergo a tax audit under Section 44AB.

Is standard deduction of ₹75,000 applicable under Section 44ADA?

No. Standard deduction is available only to salaried employees and pensioners under Section 16(ia). Freelancers and independent consultants receive the 50% presumptive expense deduction instead.

Which ITR form is filed for Section 44ADA?

Freelancers and professionals utilizing Section 44ADA must file ITR-4 (Sugam) on the Income Tax e-filing portal.
